The story

Priya owns Acme Studio, and Lumen Café since PR 4. Dev, who owns Northwind Bakery, sends her the join link /join/northwind-bakery so she can help with their Christmas menu. Today that page only makes new accounts, and Priya's email is refused with "An account with this email already exists". After PR 6 she can ask to join with the account she has.

1 · Open the linkSigned in: "Ask to join Northwind Bakery" with her username filled in. Signed out: sign in with her password, or get an email link.
2 · AskA pending membership attaches to her account. She lands on Home in Acme Studio with a notice. The switcher shows Northwind as "Waiting for approval".
3 · Dev approvesOn Members, Priya's row looks like anyone else's: username and email. Approving revokes any old tokens she had for Northwind.

1. The join page, signed out

One page for new and existing people, with two tabs: "New here" and "I have an account". The person picks. The server never says which one fits, so the page reveals nothing about the email.

Desktop · 1440 · "I have an account", filled in

Recommended shape: two tabs, two proofs

  1. I have an account: email and password, then "Sign in and continue". This is a normal sign-in (same attempt limits, same "Email or password is incorrect."), which then shows the signed-in page in section 3. Nothing attaches until she presses "Ask to join" there.
  2. New here: email only, then "Email me a link". Everyone gets the same "Check your email" page. The email itself differs: a new address gets "finish joining", an existing account gets "join with your account" (section 2). Only the inbox owner sees the difference.
  3. "Email me a link instead" under the password is for people who have an account but not their password to hand. It sends the same email from the same form, without a password.
  4. The tabs are links (/join/northwind-bakery and ?with=password), so they work without JavaScript and the back button behaves.
  5. "New here" is the default. Most people who open a join link are new to the app.

Why not the other shapes

  • Keep today's form, plus "Already have an account? Sign in". Today's form makes the account and signs you in on the spot, and refuses a known email. Both tell a stranger whether the email has an account. To fix that, the new-account path has to answer the same for every email, which means an email step. That is the "New here" tab.
  • Email first, then a fork. The second step can't depend on the email without revealing it, so it would show both choices anyway. One extra step for everyone, for the same result.

The cost: brand-new people now go through their inbox before they can ask to join. Today they are in the waiting room straight away. In return, every joiner's email is proven. Today anyone can join with someone else's address. See question 1.

Copy

  • Heading: "Ask to join Northwind Bakery". The button on every path is "Ask to join", because joining needs an owner's yes. Today's page says "Request to join" and "Request access"; this replaces both.
  • "Check your email" names the address she typed, so a typo is easy to spot. It says the link works once, for 60 minutes.
  • It never says "if an account exists". Every address gets an email. The only exception is the send limit (3 per address, 10 per IP, per hour, as for "Forgot password"). Over it, the page is the same and nothing is sent.

Decisions

  1. The link doesn't sign her in. It only attaches a pending membership. A link that signs you in would be a new way in that skips the password and the account lock. She signs in as usual afterwards. See question 3.
  2. If this browser is already signed in as Priya, "Ask to join" lands on Home with the notice from section 5, as the signed-in path does.
  3. Signed out, it lands on "Request sent", with "Sign in" for when she wants it.
  4. The username starts with her account's name, as on the signed-in page.
  5. For a new address, the link page is today's join form without the email field: username and password. "Ask to join" makes the account, signs in and shows today's waiting page.
  6. If the account is already in Northwind (active, waiting, declined or removed), the link page shows the same state as the signed-in page does (section 4).

Priya opens Dev's link while signed in. Today she would be sent straight to her dashboard. Now she sees one question with her username filled in.

Decisions

  1. The auth layout, not the app layout. A centred card, as on today's join and waiting pages. It doesn't belong to any one of her organisations, and it works for someone whose only membership is pending or removed.
  2. "Signed in as" with her email and "Not you? Sign out", because a shared laptop could be signed in as someone else.
  3. The username starts with her username in the org this browser is in (here "priya", from Acme Studio). Same hint as PR 4: "How people in Northwind Bakery see you."
  4. Two plain facts: who decides and what they see (username and email), and that nothing from Acme Studio is shared.
  5. "Cancel" goes to Home. Nothing changes.
  6. "Ask to join" is a plain form post with no org claim: it acts on the account, not on the current org. It never switches org.

6. When Dev approves

Today an approval sends nothing: no notification and no email (docs/flows/notifications.md: "Joining requests, approvals, removals and role changes send none"). A new person finds out by pressing "Check again" on the waiting page. Priya isn't on that page. She is working in Acme Studio, so she needs to be told there.

Desktop · 1440 · next visit to Home in Acme Studio

Recommended

  1. The switcher row goes live on her next full page load, anywhere: Northwind moves up into the list, A to Z, with "Member". No new code, since the list is read on every load.
  2. A one-time notice on Home, in whichever org she is in: "Northwind Bakery let you in." with "Open Northwind Bakery". It shows until she opens Northwind or closes it.
  3. Not a bell notification. A notification belongs to one org, and her bell counts only the org she is in. One filed under Northwind would sit unseen until she opened Northwind, which is what it is meant to prompt.
  4. Nothing live. The notice waits for her next Home load, as the switcher row does.
  5. Open sessions get Northwind straight away. She doesn't sign in again.
Question for Raj 5: how she hears about itRecommended: switcher plus the one-time Home notice. It needs one new timestamp on the membership (for example approval_seen_at), so a small migration. An email "You're in Northwind Bakery" would reach her when she isn't using the app, but approvals email nobody today, new people included. Suggest it separately for everyone.

8. Edge cases

Wrong password
Too many tries
Locked account, right password

Signing in on the join page

  • The same sign-in as /login: the attempt is recorded first, an unknown email still runs a hash, and the same three messages. None of them reveals an account to someone without the password.
  • After a wrong password the email stays, the password clears, and "Email me a link instead" is right there.
  • A locked account can still use "Email me a link instead", and the request attaches. She can't sign in to see it until she resets her password, as for any locked account.
Super admin acting in Acme
Link used or expired
Link opened while signed in as someone else

Super admin

  • Raj is acting in Acme Studio and opens Northwind's link. The page says the request comes from his own account, and that asking ends acting.
  • Asking clears acting_org, as creating an org does in PR 4. He lands on Home in his own org with the section 5 notice.
  • A super admin with no membership of his own lands on /admin instead: a waiting membership must not send him to the waiting page. See question 9.

The org cap

No cap and no screen. The 10-organisation cap counts orgs you created (organisations.created_by_id), and joining creates none. Every request still needs a yes from that org's owner. See question 10 for a daily limit on asking.

Something went wrong

Failures

  • Server error: a red alert on the card, "Something went wrong, and nothing was sent. Try again." The username stays. The ask is one transaction, so nothing half-attaches.
  • No connection: these are plain forms, like today's join and sign-in pages, so the browser shows its own offline page and the back button returns to the filled form.
  • An unknown slug: today's public 404 page.
  • The email link, used twice or after 60 minutes: "This link has expired" with "Get a new link", back to the join page. One message for both, so it doesn't say whether it was used.
  • Opened while signed in as Dawa: "This link is for another account." The page names the address the link was sent to, since whoever holds the link already has that email. Nothing attaches to Dawa.

9. Questions for Raj

Each comes with a recommendation. The mockup draws the recommended answer.

1. The signed-out shapeTwo tabs: "New here" (email link) and "I have an account" (password, or "Email me a link instead"). New people go through their inbox before they can ask.Recommended: yes. It is the only shape where the reply is the same for every email, and it proves every joiner's email, which today nothing does. The alternative, today's instant form plus a sign-in link, keeps refusing known emails and so reveals them.
2. The send limit for join emailsRecommended: reuse "Forgot password"'s 3 per address and 10 per IP per hour, with the same page whether or not the limit was hit.
3. Does the email link sign you in?Recommended: no. It only attaches a waiting membership. Signing in by link would be a new way in that skips the password and the account lock. She signs in as usual.
4. Asking again after a declineRecommended: allowed, no wait. No one is notified of a request, so it can't spam the owner.
5. How she hears she was approvedRecommended: the switcher row goes live, plus a one-time Home notice in whichever org she is in. Not a bell notification, since the bell only counts the org you are in. Needs one timestamp on memberships (a small migration). An approval email for everyone is a separate suggestion.
6. The welcome pop-up after joining with an existing accountRecommended: skip it, as PR 4 does for a new org, because she already uses the app. Brand-new joiners keep it. The "Getting started" guide still shows.
7. Telling her about a declineRecommended: yes, once, on Home: "Northwind Bakery didn't approve your request." Today's declined page already tells people with no other org, so this keeps them equal. Same timestamp as question 5.
8. "Removed on" and "Declined on" in the owner's waiting listRecommended: yes, the date only. It's the org's own history. Never show that someone has an account or orgs elsewhere.
9. Super adminRecommended: allowed. Asking ends acting, as creating an org does. With no membership of his own he lands on /admin, never the waiting page.
10. A daily limit on asking to joinRecommended: yes, 10 requests per account per 24 hours, counted from memberships created or re-opened. It stops one account filling many orgs' waiting lists. Over it: "You've asked to join 10 organisations today. Try again tomorrow." on the username field. No lifetime cap.
11. Signup still reveals emails/signup says "An account with this email already exists", so a stranger can check any email there anyway. Recommended: accept it for PR 6 and fix signup the same way in a follow-up. Otherwise the join page's care buys little.
